## A weak station no longer decodes its neighbour's mirror; Airspy at 10 MS/s

- **Fix: the same station on two channels.** On a receiver with two stations, e.g. 102.8 and 103.5, both channels could show the same station's PI and name. Seen on docker01: P4 Värmland on the 102.8 channel too.
  - The tuner was centred exactly between the two stations, so each sat on the other's mirror. Every receiver leaves a weak mirror image of each station (I/Q imbalance, 25–45 dB weaker).
  - When one station is weak or not heard, its channel decoded the other's mirror instead.
  - The centre is now chosen so that no mirror lands within 250 kHz of a station. Stations that cannot be kept apart that way get a warning in the log.
  - This applies to RTL-SDR dongles too. After the update, a channel whose station is not heard shows *no RDS* instead of its neighbour.

### Airspy at 10 MS/s (R2) and 6 MS/s (Mini)

- **`sample_rate:` in `receivers.yml` (optional, an Airspy only).** `10M` on an Airspy R2 or `6M` on an Airspy Mini lets the receiver's stations be up to **7.8 MHz** or **4.6 MHz** apart, up to **8 stations**:

  ```yaml
    - serial: "0x62CC68FF3584805B"
      sample_rate: 10M
      frequencies: [99.5M, 102.8M, 106.9M]
  ```

  Without it, an Airspy covers 2 MHz and 4 stations, as before. A rate the device does not have (`6M` on an R2) stops the receiver with a message. On an RTL-SDR the setting is ignored, with a warning.
- **A shared FFT:** at these rates all stations share one FFT per block, and each takes its own part of it straight down to 250 kHz. More stations cost little extra CPU. Decoding, recording and the aggregator see the same 171 kHz multiplex as before.
- **Lost samples are reported.** When the host cannot keep up with `airspy_rx`, the log says so, at most once a minute: `Streaming at 8.2 MSPS, not 10: samples are lost`.
- **The calibration at these rates** decodes each capture as it comes in, instead of holding hundreds of MB of IQ. Changing `sample_rate:` calibrates the receiver again.
- **The aggregator keeps 8 stations per receiver** (was 4) **and 32 per node** (was 8).

Untested on hardware: whether the host keeps up with 10 MS/s (40 MB/s over USB), stations near the edge (±3.9 MHz from the centre), and one gain for stations of very different strength.

Update with `docker compose pull && docker compose up -d`.
